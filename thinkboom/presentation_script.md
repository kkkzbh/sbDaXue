# Presentation Script (Shortened for 3-4 Minutes, with Transitions)

## English Version (For Presentation)

**(Slide 1: Title Slide)**

Hello everyone, thank you for coming.

Today, I'll be talking about our research on how AI programming tools affect student learning in computer science.

**(Slide 2 & 3: Introduction & Motivation)**

**First, let's set the stage with a brief introduction.**

While AI tools are powerful, it's critical to understand their impact. We want to help students use them effectively and avoid problems like becoming too dependent on the AI.

This concern leads to our central research question: Do these AI tools *really* help students learn better, code better, and feel more confident?

**(Slide 5, 6, 7: Methods - The Core of the Talk)**

**So, how did we go about answering this question? This brings us to the core of our study: the Methods.**

Our experiment used a classic pre-test, post-test design.
1.  First, we tested all students' initial skills.
2.  Then, we divided them into two groups: a **control group** using standard tools, and a **treatment group** using AI-assisted tools.
3.  Both groups learned for four weeks before we gave them a final post-test.
4.  Finally, we compared the results to see the difference.

To measure their progress, we assessed three key areas: their conceptual knowledge, their programming performance on a final project, and their self-confidence in coding. Our analysis uses standard statistical tests to find any significant differences in learning between the two groups.

**(Slide 8: Expected Contributions)**

**Moving on from our methodology, what do we hope to achieve with this work?** We expect our findings will offer valuable, evidence-based guidelines for educators on how to best integrate AI tools into their classrooms.

**(Slide 9: Thank You)**

**To wrap things up,** this research aims to provide clear insights into using AI in education.

This brings me to the end of my presentation. Thank you for listening.
